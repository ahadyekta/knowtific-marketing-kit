## 1. Why marketing should support sales

The rule: **the closer you are to the dollars, the more you earn.** Supporting sales is the fastest way for a marketer to become valuable. When marketing helps move revenue, sales leaders' attitude to marketing changes completely, and your other ideas and budget requests get approved more easily.

Two situations:
- **Selling to retailers**: marketing supports the sales team getting products stocked and moving off shelves (see partners-events-retail.md).
- **Lead-based businesses** (services, B2B, high-ticket): marketing has a direct, big impact on how the sales team performs.

## 2. Leads and the CRM

A **lead** is someone who asked for information (a consultation form, a demo order, a quote request) whom sales is trying to convert. They become a **customer** when closed. The **CRM** (e.g. HubSpot, or a custom dashboard) logs every lead: status, source, date, owner, number of contacts, likelihood to close, size, notes.

## 3. Collateral by stage

| Stage | What to have ready |
|---|---|
| **Right after enquiry** | Thank-you page (video from the person who'll call, what happens next); first email from the salesperson with key assets |
| **During the call** | A short deck, examples of work, case studies, pricing ranges |
| **After the call** | A follow-up pack that's easy to forward. Often the person you spoke to must convince a partner or boss: give them the numbers, comparison, reasons, and similar customers' results so they can forward it and get approval |
| **Later: upsell** | After about 90 days a client evaluates you. Have results summaries and next-step offers ready to sell more |

## 4. Collateral types

- **FAQ and objection answers**: a PDF, a section in the email, or a personable video from the owner or CEO
- **Testimonials and case studies**: collect every positive review; one-page case study PDFs (problem, what we did, timeline, result)
- **Best content**: high-performing or viral videos ("check out our recent video, it's going viral"). In retail, proof of attention shows buyers that customers will come looking
- **Infographics and data**: industry trends, results, savings, quick stats a salesperson can rattle off
- **Comparisons**: how you compare with alternatives, in a simple table or graphic someone can paste and send

**Marketing owns the design.** Marketing sees all the assets, knows what performs and has creative talent. Set up a request process (a form in your project tool, assigned to a designer, reviewed regularly) and review what sales is sending out.

**It's a flywheel.** A good testimonial video becomes a sales asset, an email in the automation, an ad, and an idea for more content.

## 5. Making the most of leads

**Speed of response.** The faster sales touches a new lead, the higher the close rate. Called within 60 seconds of signing up, a lead is warm and remembers you. Two days later they ask "which webinar was that?" Push for speed and monitor it.

**Number of touches.** Don't give up after one call or email. One high-ticket outdoor business (average order about $9,000) found sales clustered at the first or second touch, or around the **11th**. Knowing that, salespeople kept going. Give every touch a reason: new content, a timely moment (a holiday, a season relevant to the niche), a case study, an answer to a common worry, a genuine offer.

| Touch | Example reason |
|---|---|
| 1 | Instant reply + call within minutes |
| 2 | Same day: SMS with "what happens next" and a review link |
| 3 | Day 1–2: quote + FAQ PDF |
| 4 | Day 3: case study similar to their job |
| 5 | Day 7: call to answer questions |
| 6+ | Fortnightly/monthly: new content, seasonal reminder, availability update |

**Triage.** Find hidden leads and pass them to sales:
- A company running an employee LinkedIn program can post qualified commenters and new followers in a Slack channel for sales to connect with ("Mind if I connect you with Corey, who can tell you more?").
- A roofing company with 2,000 Instagram followers can check which followers live in the service area and DM them: "Saw you follow us. Thinking about a new roof anytime soon?"

## 6. DM selling

- Reply to every comment and DM with something personal.
- If a commenter looks like a real prospect, DM them: ask a question, offer help, suggest a call. Don't paste a pitch.
- Encourage replies with Instagram Stories ("Anyone planning a bathroom this year? Reply and I'll send our price guide").
- Manage DM conversations in the CRM. Businesses that sell at volume through DMs run this as a "DM funnel".
- Get people from DMs onto calls with sales as fast as possible.
- Commercial messages, including DMs, should follow the Spam Act basics (see australia-compliance.md).

## 7. Working with the sales team

- **Find the hungriest salesperson.** Not necessarily the best one (top performers can be hard to work with), but the one who wants to improve and earn more. Work closely with them.
- Map the funnel together (see funnels-and-offers.md).
- Share the hottest content every week so salespeople feel part of a movement.
- Measure what sales does with marketing's leads: it decides how much value your work creates.

## 8. Learning from sales calls

- Record sales calls (Granola, Fathom and similar note-takers).
- Every week, feed the notes to AI and ask: What objections came up? What content should we make to handle them? How can marketing better prepare sales? Which customer pain points should our website copy reinforce?
- Turn the answers into FAQ content, ads, emails, and new landing page sections.

## 9. One-to-many selling

Webinars, community calls and live workshops let one person pitch to many: present, answer questions, make an offer. Good when you can't do enough one-on-one calls, or have a charismatic presenter. Reminders by email and SMS lift attendance.

## 10. Outbound

Outbound means contacting prospects first (cold email, LinkedIn messages) instead of waiting for them. It's hard and takes persistence ("sheer will and attrition"), but it's the channel you control when nothing else is working. One agency was built on hundreds of emails a day plus dozens of LinkedIn messages.

**Tools**
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator**: paid; find the right people and message them
- **Cold email platforms** (ListKit, Apollo and similar): handle extra sending domains and inboxes, warm-up (so emails don't land in spam), lead databases and automation. Never send cold email from the main business domain

**Principles**
- A tight target list beats volume: right industry, size, role, location.
- Personalise the first line with something specific and true (their website, a review, a recent post).
- Offer value first: a short audit, an idea, a relevant example.
- Short sequences: 3–5 touches over 2–3 weeks; stop on reply.
- Comply with the Spam Act: identify yourself, include an unsubscribe, have a genuine reason to contact the business (see australia-compliance.md).

**For Knowtific's own growth:** target local businesses (tradies, clinics) with an observation about their website or Google Business Profile, e.g. a quick video review or a mobile-speed screenshot, plus one concrete fix. Pair with a free audit lead magnet. Use the outbound skill to plan and write sequences.
