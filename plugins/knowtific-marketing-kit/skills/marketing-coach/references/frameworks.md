## 1. The five jobs of marketing

| Job | What it covers | Local example |
|---|---|---|
| Brand | How people feel about the business; reputation and personality | "The physio that actually explains your injury" |
| Product / offer | What makes the service different and how it's packaged | "Fixed price before we start" |
| Content | Useful or interesting things you publish | "3 signs your switchboard is unsafe" video |
| Demand | Paid ads and outbound that create leads now | Meta ad: "Free bathroom quote this week" |
| Ops | CRM, tracking, automation, AI tools | Pixel, lead form → CRM → instant SMS |

A strong small-business marketer understands all five and is genuinely good at two or three.

## 2. Positioning

Positioning is the one clear idea a business is known for, the reason to choose it over the alternatives. Generic claims ("quality service, friendly team, competitive prices") fail because every competitor says them.

**Shelf test:** picture the business in a row of competitors (a Google results page, a Maps pack, an Instagram feed). Why would someone pick this one?

**AI-legible test:** would ChatGPT or Google's AI answer have a specific reason to recommend this business for a specific query? ("Best physio for running injuries in Melbourne's east" → a clinic that clearly says it specialises in runners.)

### Positioning types

| Type | Idea | Local example | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Performance | Measurably better | "Most hot water systems replaced same day" | Claims must be true and provable |
| New form | A known thing in a new format | Mobile physio that comes to your home | Must explain the format clearly |
| Counter-position | Fix what people hate about the usual option | "No call-out fee" / "Book online in 30 seconds" | Pick a real, common annoyance |
| Smart | Technology in a boring category | Live "tech on the way" tracking link | Tech must help the customer, not just look modern |
| Value | Cheaper, close enough in quality | New agency or new clinic winning first clients | Hard to sustain; easy to undercut |
| Niche | Made for a specific group | Physio for runners; accountant for tradies; Farsi-speaking dentist | Smaller market, stronger loyalty |
| Life stage / generation | Same service aimed at one age group | Strength training for over-50s | Messaging and imagery must match the group |
| Own one word | Be the business for one quality | Electrician who owns "on time" (late = free call-out) | Must deliver it every time |
| Premium: expert | Built for serious users | Tradie tools brand for professionals | Keep proof of expertise visible |
| Premium: design | People pay for beauty | High-end custom kitchens | Needs excellent photography |
| Premium: custom | Made for you | Tailored renovations | Process must feel personal |
| Premium: smart luxury | Luxury quality without the luxury markup | High-spec bathrooms at mid-range prices | Explain why it's cheaper |

Positioning comes first. Every ad, post, page and email should repeat it. If a client has none, finding it is the first job, before any ad spend.

## 3. Brand world map

A planning board with four areas. Fill each one in, then look for the gaps.

1. **Characters:** who can speak for the brand? Owner, staff experts, happy customers, partners (suppliers, designers, referrers), outside creators.
2. **Sets:** where can you film? Permanent (showroom, clinic, workshop, van) and temporary (job sites before/during/after, events, trade shows).
3. **Events calendar:**
   - Company events: launches, openings, anniversaries, new services
   - Culture events: what customers are thinking about at that time. In Australia: New Year projects, school holidays, EOFY (June), footy finals, spring, Christmas ("finish before guests arrive"), summer heat, winter heating.
   - Industry events: trade shows, rule changes, rebates, competitor launches
4. **Plot:**
   - Enemies: what competitors do and how the business responds
   - Friends: partners to team up with for joint content or offers
   - Specials: events the business invents to fill quiet months (e.g. a 4-part "winter makeover" series)

**Gaps to look for:** nobody willing to be on camera; nowhere good to film; empty months in the calendar; no partners.

**Service idea:** run this as a paid strategy workshop, with the fee credited against a monthly retainer if the client signs.

## 4. Positioning statement and copy styles

### One-sentence positioning statement

> For [who] who [need or problem], [business] is the [category] that [main benefit]. Unlike [alternatives], we [key difference].

Mission, vision and taglines matter much less than this one sentence. It is the most useful single input for AI tools writing for the brand.

### Copy styles

| Style | Feel | Best for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct response | Urgent, names the problem, specific numbers, clear ask | Most service ads; often the best performer | "Leaking shower? Fixed in 1 day or it's free. Book before Friday." |
| Evidence-led | Calm, proof, reviews, process | Health, high-trust, high-price services | "400+ bathrooms. 4.9★ from 230 Google reviews." |
| Story-driven | Starts with a person or moment | Testimonials (where allowed), brand content | "Mark called us at 7am. His only bathroom had flooded…" |
| Culturally savvy | Trends, humour, pop culture | Young, very online audiences | Usually not for trades |

For most local service businesses, test direct response against evidence-led first.

## 5. AI maturity levels

1. Chatting: one-off questions
2. Project: a workspace loaded with the brand's context, so every answer fits the brand
3. Skills: saved instructions for tasks you repeat
4. Agents: AI that runs tasks on a schedule (weekly ad summary, lead follow-up)
5. Brain: one system holding ads, sales, costs and content data that supports decisions

Focus on levels 2 and 3 and share them with the whole team. What goes in the project: positioning statement, service details, offers, proof (reviews, results), objections and answers. Long brand-guideline PDFs add little.

## 6. Organic social

**Pick one main platform and get good at it.** Cross-post everything to the others.

- **Instagram:** best main platform for most local businesses. Three approaches:
  1. Expert on camera (recommended for most): one person talks to camera about the topic, 3+ posts a week
  2. Story/series: the real story of the business, about 2 a week; needs a genuinely interesting story
  3. Art-directed: every post a crafted piece; needs strong design talent; suits beauty/fashion/design
- **TikTok:** fast reach but business accounts often struggle; reuse Instagram videos unless you have a specialist.
- **YouTube:** excellent for service businesses answering common questions; even 1–2k views per video can bring good leads. Titles and thumbnails matter a lot.
- **LinkedIn:** for B2B; works best when several people from the same business post and engage.

**Content funnel:** top (attention, no selling), middle (teach and build trust), bottom (direct ask: "3 spots left this month"). You need all three.

**Rules:**
- People beat brand accounts. Faces build trust.
- The competition is everything else in the feed, not other plumbers. Make a fresh account, follow what the ideal customer follows, scroll it daily for two weeks.
- Ask: why would someone send this to a friend? (Relatable, useful, surprising, beautiful.)

**Measure:** per post: hook rate, views, saves, shares (most important). Per month: reach, leads, sales traced to social.

**Pillar system:**
1. Choose 3–5 formats (pillars), e.g. tips to camera, before/after, myth vs fact carousel, behind the scenes.
2. Post each 3–4 times a month.
3. Review monthly: compare pillars on shares, saves, hook rate, followers gained. Replace any pillar that underperforms two months running.
4. Send a short monthly summary to the client.

Expect traction in 3–5 months. When a client suggests a new idea, add it as a test pillar for a month and let the numbers decide.

## 7. Paid ads and the numbers

### Key numbers

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ROAS | Revenue ÷ ad spend. 2 ROAS = $2 back per $1 spent |
| CPA / CPL | Cost per sale / cost per lead |
| CAC | Cost to win one new customer |
| LTV | Total a customer spends over time |
| Close rate | % of leads that become paying jobs |
| CTR, CPC, CPM, hook rate | Diagnostic signals, not goals |

**Service business maths:** affordable cost per customer = job gross profit × share you're willing to spend. Affordable CPL = affordable cost per customer × close rate. Use `/knowtific-marketing-kit:ads-math`.

**First-order profit vs LTV:** businesses with little cash need the first job to pay for the ad. Businesses with repeat customers can pay more upfront. A common target is LTV ≈ 3× CAC.

### What makes ads work (in order)
1. Right customer + right message (positioning)
2. Which service you lead with (the one that already sells best)
3. Price point (each business has a sweet spot; bundle if too cheap)
4. Offer (free inspection, fixed-price quote in 24h, first visit $49)
5. Creative (the image/video and its first 3 seconds)

### Platform order
- E-commerce: Meta first, then TikTok (reuse Meta ads), YouTube, Google.
- **Local services: Google Search often comes first**, because urgent needs are searched (burst pipe, broken hot water). Meta creates demand for things people don't search yet (renovations, batteries, "fix your back pain"). Many need both.
- LinkedIn ads: only B2B or high-ticket. Newsletter sponsorships: underrated for niches.

### Meta setup basics
- Campaign → ad sets → ads. Objective: Sales (shops) or Leads (services).
- Start with ad-set budgets (you control spend per idea) rather than campaign budgets, until the account has lots of data.
- One concept per ad set; 3–10 genuinely different ads inside it.
- Name ad sets with audience + creative type (e.g. `Homeowners45+ | Owner-to-camera`).
- Pixel and conversion tracking must work first. Prefer a dedicated landing page.
- Targeting: lookalikes (start ~3%, widen) → broad once the account has data. Interests only when there's no data.

### Weekly testing loop
Plan new creative → launch → read results → plan next week. Small accounts: 2–3 new ad sets a week.

**Kill/scale rule:** set a target CPL. Spend 3–5× the target on an ad set before judging.
- Hitting target → increase budget (scale)
- Close → keep testing a little longer
- Clicks/engagement but no leads → the ad works; fix the landing page or offer
- Clearly bad → turn off

**AI with ads:** use AI to read results, find winners and losers, suggest hooks and mock up ideas. Publish ads yourself; mass automated ad creation can get accounts flagged.

## 8. Outbound

Contacting prospects directly by cold email or LinkedIn. Reliable when nothing else is working; it's a numbers game. Tools: LinkedIn Sales Navigator, cold email platforms that handle extra domains and inbox warm-up, lead databases.

For an agency: email local businesses a short, specific observation about their website or Google profile. Follow the Spam Act (see compliance file).

## 9. Creators and influencers

- **Creator:** makes good content; follower count matters less. Pay for the video.
- **Influencer:** has an audience that trusts them. Check the audience is local.

**Ways to use them:** make ads (real people talking usually beat polished brand ads; use different ages and styles); gifting (best for cheap products; for services, invite to experience it); affiliate (commission or referral fee per booking); recurring face of the brand's content.

**Finding them:** start with existing customers who post or tag the business; search Instagram/TikTok by local hashtags and location; marketplaces (Collabstr, Influee, JoinBrands, The Right Fit in Australia); platform creator tools.

**Brief (keep it modular):**
1. Two key messages only
2. Target audience
3. Content type and how much freedom they have
4. Hook ideas: on-screen text, visual, first spoken line
5. Reference videos
6. Format (fast list, story, demo)
7. Simple outline: hook → problem → solution → result → CTA
8. Assets (photos, B-roll, logos)
9. Versions: 2–3 hooks, one organic ending, one ad ending

**Agreement:** raw files, partnership ad code, usage rights (where and how long), edit permission, price. Creators usually charge extra for ad usage.

**Partnership ads (whitelisting):** the ad runs from the creator's account using an ad code; the business controls budget and targeting. "Dark" partnership ads run without being posted to the creator's feed.

## 10. Funnel, landing pages and follow-up

**Flow:** attention → call to action → landing page → lead or sale → CRM → follow-up → customer.

- **CTA:** every piece of content tells people what to do next. Instagram bio: what you do / why follow / CTA + link.
- **Landing page:** one page built for one action. Conversion rate changes cost per lead dramatically (100 clicks at $1: 1% = $100 CPL, 4% = $25, 12% ≈ $8).
- **Service page structure:** hero (headline, top benefits, CTA) → real results → benefits from the customer's view → how it works → what's included → proof → team → reviews → FAQ/objections → final CTA.
- **Listicle page:** teach first ("7 mistakes…"), then sell. For people not ready yet.
- **Luxury page:** answer "why is it worth this much?" with craft, detail and process.
- **Habit:** whenever you click an ad, note the landing page's sections. Build a swipe file.

**After the lead:**
- **Speed to touch:** contact within 1–5 minutes. Instant SMS acknowledgement, owner notification, or AI reply.
- **Thank-you page:** short video from the person who'll call, what happens next, links to proof.
- **Close assets:** best videos, reviews, FAQ/objection answers, case studies.
- **Learn from calls:** record calls, pull out objections weekly, turn them into content.

**The two numbers to obsess over:** landing page conversion rate and lead close rate. Also watch lead volume and qualified rate.

Map the flow with the people who answer the phones; they know the objections.

## 11. Campaigns

A campaign focuses all channels on one idea at one time, so the effect is bigger than separate posts. Types: theme campaigns (seasonal), launches with an anchor person, bundled feature/update launches, a yearly theme.

**Stages:** teaser → campaign → unveil (first proper explanation; great evergreen content) → sustain (answer questions, refresh later).

For each stage plan internal channels (social, email/SMS, website, physical location, ads) and external (partners, resellers, creators, local media). Then an asset list with owner and due date, working back from launch.

**Roles:** brand strategy = what we stand for; creative direction = big creative ideas; creative strategy = weekly ad concepts, briefs, feedback and results.

## 12. PR and crisis response

Traditional press coverage rarely pays off for small businesses. PR still matters for problems: a viral bad review, a mistake, a complaint. Lay out the options (apologise and fix, explain calmly with facts, contact privately, stay quiet), predict how people will react to each, then choose.

## 13. Email and SMS

- **Campaign:** one message to many people. **Flow:** automatic messages triggered by an action.
- Send 1–3 emails a week for most businesses. Daily discounting trains people to wait for deals.
- **Campaign types to rotate:** reuse best content; new service; community/customer highlight; business update from a real person; things that go together; industry curation; FAQ; deal.
- **Small and service businesses:** plain-text emails from the owner's name get replies, which start sales conversations.
- **Core flows (e-commerce):** welcome, abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase.
- **Core flows (services):** enquiry → welcome + what happens next; quote sent → follow-ups (e.g. day 1, 3, 7) that stop on reply; job done → thanks + review request; 6–12 months → service reminder.
- **Segments:** split the list by need (runners vs office workers vs older patients) and send each relevant content.
- **Weekly routine:** review last week's content, news and sales questions → reuse them as this week's emails.
- Research others' emails on Milled.

## 14. Sales support

The closer marketing is to revenue, the more valuable it is.

- **Collateral by stage:** right after enquiry (thank-you page, first email); during the call (examples, short deck); after the call (pricing, comparison, reviews, case study); later (upsell).
- Often the person enquiring must convince a partner or boss. Give them a package they can forward.
- **Persistence:** many buyers convert at contact 1–2, many others much later. Don't stop after one call.
- **Hidden leads:** followers in the service area who never enquired; a friendly DM can open a conversation.
- Work closely with the most motivated salesperson, not necessarily the best one.

## 15. Weekly report

One email a week to the boss or client:
1. Wins (with numbers)
2. Next (what happens this week)
3. Blockers (what you need from them)
4. Stats (leads, CPL, spend, response time, close rate, traffic)

It makes the work visible, builds trust, warns early about delays, and keeps clients longer.
