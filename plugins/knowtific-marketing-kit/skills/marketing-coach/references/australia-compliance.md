# Australian compliance notes

Practical flags, not legal advice. When something is high-risk or unclear, tell the user to check the official guidance or get legal advice. Rules change; check current versions before relying on specifics. (Last reviewed October 2026.)

## Spam Act 2003 (email, SMS, instant messages)
- Applies to commercial electronic messages: email, SMS, MMS and instant messages (which can include commercial DMs on social platforms).
- Need consent: express (they opted in) or inferred (e.g. an existing business relationship, or a conspicuously published work address relevant to the message's purpose).
- Every message must clearly identify the sender and include a working, free unsubscribe (email link; "Reply STOP" for SMS). Honour unsubscribes promptly (within 5 working days).
- Keep records of how and when consent was given.
- Cold B2B email still needs a genuine basis plus identification and unsubscribe. Treat bulk cold outreach cautiously; use separate sending domains and keep volumes reasonable.
- Regulator: ACMA.

## SMS Sender ID Register (from 1 July 2026)
- Branded alphanumeric sender names (e.g. "SmithPlumb") must be registered on ACMA's Sender ID Register, through the SMS provider. Messages with unregistered names have the name **replaced with "Unverified"** and are grouped into one "Unverified" thread on the phone, alongside likely scams. ACMA describes this as relabelling, not blocking, but it badly hurts trust and response.
- Sending from a normal or virtual mobile number isn't affected and lets customers reply.
- The standard registration path is for businesses with an ABN; ACMA has a separate process for those without one.
- **Agencies sending for a client** (e.g. Knowtific running a client's follow-up SMS) are "entity associates": the client owns the sender ID, registers it or confirms the application, and must authorise each SMS provider that sends with it. Set this up early; ACMA recommends contacting SMS providers well before launch.

## Telemarketing and the Do Not Call Register (phone outreach)
- Before cold calling consumers (and any number on the register), check it against the **Do Not Call Register** unless you have the person's consent. Washing lists is done through donotcall.gov.au.
- The Telemarketing Industry Standard sets **calling hours** (weekdays 9am–8pm, Saturday 9am–5pm; no calls on Sundays or national public holidays), and requires callers to identify themselves and the business, give a contact number, and end the call when asked. Check the current standard before running a calling campaign.
- Regulator: ACMA.

## Health practitioners (AHPRA / National Law)
Applies to registered professions: physiotherapy, chiropractic, osteopathy, dental, psychology, podiatry, optometry, medical, nursing, occupational therapy, and others.
- **No testimonials or patient reviews about clinical aspects** (results, outcomes, diagnosis, treatment) in advertising, including on websites and social media the practice controls, and in influencer content the practice pays for.
- No false, misleading or deceptive claims; no creating an unreasonable expectation of benefit; no encouraging unnecessary use of services.
- Be careful with before/after images, "best/leading/specialist" claims (only use "specialist" if registered as one), guarantees, time-limited offers that pressure people, and unproven claims.
- Safer content: education, process ("what happens at your first visit"), team qualifications, clinic environment, non-clinical experience (parking, booking ease, friendliness).
- Unregistered allied health roles have lighter rules, but consumer law still applies.

## Therapeutic goods (TGA)
- Advertising of therapeutic goods (supplements, medical devices, some skincare with therapeutic claims) must follow the TGA Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code. Restrictions apply to health claims, testimonials and influencer endorsements (paid influencers generally can't give testimonials for therapeutic goods). Check before running product clients' health claims.

## Australian Consumer Law (ACCC)
- **Claims must be true and provable.** "Best", "fastest", "cheapest", "#1", statistics and comparisons need evidence you can produce. "Softest hoodie" or "most absorbable" style claims need substantiation.
- **Comparative advertising** is allowed but must be accurate and fair.
- **Reviews:** fake reviews, reviews written or incentivised without disclosure, or selectively hiding negative reviews in a misleading way are prohibited. Don't buy reviews. Asking every customer for an honest review is fine.
- **Prices:** if you show any part of a price, also show the **single total price** at least as prominently. Only charges that can't be worked out upfront (e.g. some delivery costs) can be described instead of included. Don't advertise "was/now" prices that weren't genuine.
- **"Free"** must genuinely be free, without hidden conditions.
- **Guarantees:** you can offer extra guarantees (e.g. money back if late), but you can't say or imply that customers lose their automatic consumer guarantees, and terms must be clear.
- **Urgency and scarcity** ("only 3 spots left") must be true.

## Google reviews (platform policy, stricter than the law)
- Google's policy **bans incentivised reviews entirely**, even when disclosed: no discounts, gifts, prize draws or freebies in exchange for a review.
- It also bans **review gating**: asking only happy customers, or screening people ("rate us first, and if it's 5 stars we'll send the Google link"). Ask every customer the same way.
- Breaking this can get reviews removed and the profile restricted. Staff and owner reviews of their own business aren't allowed either.

## Influencer and creator content
- Paid or gifted posts must be clearly disclosed (platform "Paid partnership" tag, #ad). The AANA Code of Ethics and consumer law both treat undisclosed advertising as misleading.
- Get written permission and usage rights before running a creator's content as an ad.

## Licensing and trade claims
- Trades in Victoria and other states often must show licence or registration numbers in advertising (e.g. electricians, plumbers, builders). Check the relevant regulator for the client's trade and state (in VIC: the Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC), which replaced the VBA on 1 July 2025, and Energy Safe Victoria for electrical).

## Privacy
- **Health service providers are covered by the Privacy Act whatever their turnover** (the small-business exemption doesn't apply to them). Health information is sensitive information and generally needs consent to collect. This covers clinic booking forms, intake questions and anything uploaded to ad platforms.
- Collect only what's needed; have a privacy policy if collecting personal information through forms; say how data will be used (including uploading lists to ad platforms for custom audiences); secure lead data; respect requests to access or correct data.
- Don't put health details in ad platform audiences or tracking events (e.g. a "booked: knee pain" conversion event with personal data).
- Call recording: tell people calls are recorded and check state laws.

## Quick checklist before publishing
- [ ] Every claim provable? Evidence saved?
- [ ] Health client: no clinical testimonials or outcome promises? Privacy consent on forms?
- [ ] Prices: total price shown at least as prominently as any part price? "Was" prices genuine?
- [ ] Reviews asked of everyone, with no incentive and no gating?
- [ ] Paid/gifted content disclosed?
- [ ] Licence number shown if required?
- [ ] Marketing messages have consent, sender ID (registered if branded) and unsubscribe?
- [ ] Phone outreach: DNCR checked, calling hours respected?
- [ ] Privacy policy linked near forms?
