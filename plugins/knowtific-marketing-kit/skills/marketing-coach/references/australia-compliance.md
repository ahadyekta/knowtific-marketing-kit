# Australian compliance notes

Practical flags, not legal advice. When something is high-risk or unclear, tell the user to check the official guidance or get advice. Rules change; check current versions before relying on specifics.

## Spam Act 2003 (email and SMS marketing)
- Need consent (express, or inferred from an existing relationship) before sending commercial messages.
- Every message must identify the sender and give a working, free unsubscribe (email link; "Reply STOP" for SMS). Honour unsubscribes promptly.
- Keep a record of how and when consent was given.
- Cold B2B email still needs a genuine basis (e.g. inferred consent from a published work address relevant to the role) plus identification and unsubscribe. Treat bulk cold outreach cautiously.
- Regulator: ACMA.

## SMS Sender ID Register (from 1 July 2026)
- Branded alphanumeric sender names (e.g. "SmithPlumb") must be registered through the SMS provider with ACMA; unregistered names may show as "Unverified" or be blocked.
- Sending from a normal mobile/virtual number isn't affected and lets customers reply.
- Registration requires an ABN.

## Health practitioners (AHPRA / National Law)
Applies to registered professions: physiotherapy, chiropractic, osteopathy, dental, psychology, podiatry, optometry, medical, nursing and others.
- **No testimonials or patient reviews about clinical aspects** (results, outcomes, diagnosis, treatment) in advertising, including on websites and social media the practice controls.
- No misleading claims, no unreasonable expectation of benefit, no encouraging unnecessary use of services.
- Be careful with before/after images, "best/leading" claims, guarantees, time-limited offers that pressure people, and unproven claims.
- Safer content: education, process ("what happens at your first visit"), team qualifications, clinic environment, non-clinical service experience (parking, booking ease).
- Influencer content about a practitioner's clinical results carries the same risk.
- Allied health professions that aren't registered (e.g. some exercise or massage roles) have lighter rules but consumer law still applies.

## Australian Consumer Law (ACCC)
- No misleading or deceptive claims. Statistics, "fastest", "cheapest", "guaranteed" must be true and provable.
- Fake or incentivised reviews presented as genuine are prohibited. Don't selectively hide negative reviews in a misleading way.
- Prices shown should be the total price the customer must pay (or clearly explain additional charges).
- "Free" offers must genuinely be free, without hidden conditions.

## Influencer and creator content
- Paid or gifted posts must be clearly disclosed (e.g. "Paid partnership" tag, "#ad"). The AANA Code of Ethics and consumer law both treat hidden ads as misleading.
- Get written permission and usage rights before running a creator's content as an ad.

## Licensing and trade claims
- Trades in Victoria (and elsewhere) often must show licence/registration numbers in advertising (e.g. electricians, plumbers, builders). Check the relevant regulator for the client's trade and state.

## Privacy
- Collect only what's needed; have a privacy policy if collecting personal information through forms; be careful exporting customer lists to ad platforms (custom audiences) without a proper basis and notice.
