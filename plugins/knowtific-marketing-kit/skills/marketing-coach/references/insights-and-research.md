## 1. What an insight is

An insight is a true observation about how customers think, feel or behave that the business can build on. It isn't a statistic or a demographic. Good insights explain *why* people buy or don't, and they lead to positioning, offers, content and ads that land.

- Weak: "Our customers are 35–55 homeowners."
- Insight: "Homeowners don't fear the cost of a renovation as much as the chaos: tradies not turning up, the house unusable for months."

Execution (pages, ads, content) is becoming commoditised. **The insight is what's left that's valuable**, and what makes everything else work.

## 2. Where insights come from

- **Reviews**: the client's and competitors' (Google, Facebook, product reviews). Look for repeated words, surprises, and what people mention first.
- **Support conversations and complaints**: questions that keep coming up.
- **Sales calls**: objections, hesitations, what made people say yes (recordings via Granola, Fathom).
- **Comments and DMs** on social posts and ads.
- **Search behaviour**: what people type ("cost", "vs", "worst", "how long").
- **Looking up customers**: who they are, what they post, what they follow (one fashion brand discovered many customers wore its clothes to festivals and many were influencers).
- **Staff on the tools**: tradespeople, receptionists and practitioners hear things marketing never does.
- **Culture and seasons**: ask AI what your target customers pay attention to through the year, then sanity-check it with real people.

## 3. Said vs real

There are two kinds of insight: what people know and say about themselves, and what's really going on. People convince themselves of things ("I love my house at the size it is"; "I'm not buying this to feel more confident"). Your job is to tell the difference: is this a real insight or just what people say about themselves?

Then **develop conviction**: take a clear stance ("people are lonelier and brands that create belonging win") and back it with content and advertising. In comments, some will agree and some won't; both sharpen your thinking. Taking positions publicly is how marketing works now.

## 4. The insight compendium

Keep a running list (in `marketing/insights.md`) of insights you notice in the client's market and in life generally. For each:
- The insight in one sentence
- Evidence (quotes, data, sources)
- Confidence (low/medium/high) and whether it's "said" or "real"
- How to use it: positioning, offer, content angle, ad hook, campaign concept
- Status: untested / tested (result)

Review it when planning campaigns and creative each month.

## 5. Competitor research

For each main competitor (3–5), capture:
- Positioning and main claim (homepage headline, Google Business Profile description)
- Offers and pricing signals
- Proof: review count and rating, testimonials, case studies, awards
- Common praise and complaints in their reviews (opportunities to counter-position)
- Channels: social accounts and what performs, ads running now (Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center, TikTok Creative Center), SEO terms they rank for
- Funnel: landing pages, lead magnets, follow-up speed (do a mystery-shop enquiry)
- Gaps: what nobody in the market says or does

Then answer: where's the white space? What can we credibly own?

## 6. Customer research methods

- **Five-question interviews** with recent customers: What was happening when you started looking? What else did you consider? What almost stopped you? Why us? What would you tell a friend?
- **Lost-lead calls**: why they didn't go ahead.
- **Review mining**: export reviews and have AI cluster themes and pull exact phrases for copy.
- **On-site survey** after enquiry: "What nearly stopped you from contacting us?"
- **Social listening**: local Facebook groups, Reddit, forums where customers ask for recommendations.
