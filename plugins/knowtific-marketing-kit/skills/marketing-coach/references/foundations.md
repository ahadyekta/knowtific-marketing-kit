# Foundations

The big picture of modern marketing: what the jobs are, what matters now, and how to work with AI.

## Why marketing matters more than ever

Making things got easy. Factories anywhere in the world are a search away, and AI now does the same for software. When anyone can build anything, **distribution** wins. The barriers left are **taste, distribution and attention**, which is why marketing has become one of the most wanted skills in business.

## The five jobs of marketing

| Job | What it covers | Local service example |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand marketing** | Perception, reputation, feel, purpose. Hard to track | "The physio who actually explains your injury" |
| **Product marketing** | What makes the product special and how you say it: messaging, presentation, packaging, how the product roadmap fits the brand | "Fixed price before we start" as the core offer |
| **Content marketing** | Social media, but also sales decks, collateral, guides, videos | "3 signs your switchboard is unsafe" video and a PDF checklist |
| **Demand generation** | Buying media, running ads, working with partners and agencies to create sales now | Meta and Google ads for bathroom renovation quotes |
| **Marketing ops** | The systems behind it all: CRM, automation, tracking, the website backend, AI projects and skills | Pixel + form → CRM → instant SMS |

These used to be separate people under a CMO. Now they blend: content and demand gen are merging, product marketing happens while content is made, and brand is shaped by what the world says about you. Younger marketing leaders are often hands-on individual contributors too. **The opportunity: understand all five, specialise in the 2–3 you love, and orchestrate the rest with a team and AI agents.**

## Marketing's real job, in order

1. **Differentiate.** Define what makes the business and its offers different (positioning). If this is weak, everything after it is harder.
2. **Map the toolkit.** Who can speak for the brand, where you can film, what's happening when (the brand world map).
3. **Generate demand.** Organic social, paid ads and outbound bring people in.
4. **Convert.** Landing pages, CRM, follow-up and sales support turn attention into money.
5. **Narrative.** Campaigns, email, PR and internal communication keep the story going.

Most businesses finding their footing should master steps 1–4 first. Campaign and narrative work pays off once demand and conversion work.

## Get close to the money

The most valuable marketers have taste *and* aren't afraid of numbers: they make the landing page convert better, make ads that work, and tie social media to business results. This is the real job of marketing: not to run cool campaigns or avoid being measured, but to drive leads and sales. The closer you are to the money, the more you get paid, the easier your other ideas get approved, and the better prepared you are to run your own business. Creatives often feel awkward about selling; get over it. Understanding how money flows through a business is what funds the creative work.

## Three strategy roles people confuse

| Role | Focus | Timescale | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brand strategy** | Big direction: positioning, tone of voice, which messages to test, where to put effort (creators vs retail, etc.) | A quarter, a year, a launch | "This year we target young families, not investors, and we sound friendly, not corporate" |
| **Creative strategy** | The ad account (and sometimes organic social and creators): new hooks and angles every week, written into briefs, sent to creators and designers, feedback, results | Weekly | "This week: 3 new ad sets: owner-to-camera on waiting times, a before/after carousel, a customer story" |
| **Creative direction** | The big creative vision: campaigns, shoots, packaging, merchandising. Can personally shoot, style, design, and has a network to call on | Campaign by campaign | "Winter campaign: a cosy cabin shoot with a local photographer and two creators" |

Brand strategy decides what we stand for, creative direction creates the big ideas, and creative strategy turns them into working ads and content every week. Creative strategy is a well-paid role right now because it sits next to the money.

## Insights beat execution

Building landing pages, buying media and making content are becoming commoditised: lots of people and AI tools can do them. What's left that's hard to copy is **the insight**: a true observation about how people think or behave that makes a creator's content land, a landing page convert and an ad get noticed. Keep a running compendium of insights (see `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/marketing-coach/references/insights-and-research.md`) and take clear positions on them.

## Working with AI: five levels

1. **Chatting.** Asking questions one at a time. About 90% of teams have reached this level.
2. **Project.** A shared workspace loaded with the brand's context, so every answer fits the brand. Load: the positioning statement, every product or service with details, testimonials and review exports, objections and answers, customer personas, the angles you use, and your winning scripts and ads (so new ideas build on what already worked). Skip long brand guides (most of it isn't useful to the AI). In this kit, the project's `marketing/` folder plays this role.
3. **Skills.** Saved instructions for things you do repeatedly ("every launch we make a Q&A landing page with a quiz", "every new product gets category comparison ad ideas"). This kit is a set of skills.
4. **Agents.** AI that does tasks on its own on a schedule, e.g. every Friday it reads the Meta ads account, diagnoses performance the way you would, and emails the team a summary with new ideas.
5. **Brain.** One central system connected to ads, creative, costs, product margins and content that can make business decisions with you. Few teams are here yet.

**Go multiplayer.** AI is most useful when the whole team shares it. Give named people responsibility: one person maintains the brand project (uploads new copy, feeds back what's good), another owns each skill and keeps it current. Then agents can run on top. Focus on levels 2 and 3 first; maintaining a good project and good skills is a valuable skill in itself.

**Always add the human layer.** Use AI to start, then add real case studies, things that actually happened in the business, and funny or specific details. That's the difference between mediocre AI copy and what good brands publish.

## Glossary

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| ICP | Ideal customer profile: the exact type of customer you want |
| CTA | Call to action: what you ask people to do next |
| CRM | Customer relationship manager: where leads and customers are stored and tracked |
| Lead | Someone who gave contact details but hasn't bought yet |
| Qualified lead | A lead who fits (right area, budget, need) and is likely to buy |
| Funnel | The journey from attention to purchase, where people drop off at each step |
| Top / middle / bottom of funnel | Attention / trust and education / the ask |
| Landing page | A page built for one action, where ad or social traffic lands |
| Lead magnet | Something useful given in exchange for contact details |
| Conversion rate | % of people who take the action you want |
| Close rate | % of leads who become paying customers |
| ROAS | Return on ad spend: revenue ÷ ad spend |
| CPA / CPL | Cost per action (sale) / cost per lead |
| CAC | Customer acquisition cost: cost to win one new customer |
| LTV | Lifetime value: what a customer spends with you over time |
| AOV | Average order value |
| CPM / CPC / CTR | Cost per 1,000 views / cost per click / click-through rate |
| Hook rate (thumb stop) | % of viewers who keep watching past the first seconds |
| Creative | The actual ad or content asset: image, video, copy |
| Whitelisting | Running ads from a creator's account (Meta "partnership ads") |
| Seeding | Sending free product to creators with no obligation, hoping they post |
| UGC | User-generated content: real-person style content, often paid for |
| Pillar | A repeatable content format you post regularly |
| Flow | An automated email/SMS sequence triggered by an action |
| Segment | A sub-group of your list that gets different messages |
| Setter / closer | Sales roles: the setter books calls, the closer runs them and closes |
| Tripwire | A cheap first offer that leads into a bigger one |
| VSL | Video sales letter: a long persuasive video that sells |
| MCP | A connection that lets AI read (and sometimes change) another app's data |
