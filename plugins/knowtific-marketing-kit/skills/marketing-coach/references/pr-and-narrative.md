## 1. PR today

Getting a journalist to write about you works much less than it used to. For most small businesses it isn't worth much time or money compared to social, ads and partnerships. Still useful: local news for genuinely newsworthy stories (community projects, milestones, awards), trade media for B2B credibility, podcasts, and links that help SEO.

## 2. Crisis communication

Where PR still really matters: when something goes wrong online. A viral bad review, a mistake in an ad, an accusation against the founder, an employee incident. In the internet era you're in a constant communication flow, and the response decides the outcome.

**Process**
1. **Pause and gather facts.** What actually happened? Who's affected? Is it spreading, and where?
2. **List the options:**
   - Apologise publicly and fix it
   - Explain calmly with facts and stand firm
   - Contact the person privately and resolve it
   - Stay quiet (sometimes right for small, unfair complaints that aren't spreading)
   - Combination (reply briefly in public, resolve in private)
3. **Role-play each option.** Ask AI to act as a crisis PR advisor with the brand's persona and what people are saying, then think as a human: how will customers, staff and critics react to each option? What could people get angry about? What counterbalance can we create?
4. **Choose and act quickly**, with one clear owner and approved wording.
5. **Follow up:** fix the root cause, tell people what changed, watch sentiment.

**Example:** a builder gets a viral 1-star review. Options: reply calmly with the facts and an offer to fix it; call the customer privately; or ignore it. Each has consequences. Usually: a short, calm public reply plus a private call works best.

Rules: never argue in comments, never reveal private customer details, don't delete legitimate criticism (it backfires and can be misleading), get legal advice for defamation or regulatory issues, and for health practitioners follow AHPRA obligations.

## 3. The weekly internal email

The single habit that most improves a marketer's career. Marketing is hard for non-marketers to understand. What bosses and clients want is confidence that you know what you're doing, that it produces measurable return, and that you care. One email every week:

1. **Wins**: what went well, with numbers. Nobody notices marketing wins unless you point them out; cheerlead your own and your team's.
2. **Next**: what you're working on next week, in priority order. People know your priorities and can add or challenge them.
3. **Blockers**: what's stopping you and what you need from whom. Problems are flagged weeks early, not at the last second, and expectations are clear.
4. **Stats**: the key numbers (leads, CPL, ROAS, spend, response time, close rate, views, list growth). Pull them from ad and email connections. People may not read them, but they see that you measure.

**Roll-up for teams.** Team leads (design, content, e-commerce, media buying, events) each send wins/next/blockers/stats to the marketing head, who picks the biggest wins, biggest blockers and most important stats and sends a roll-up to the executive team. It also lets the head advocate for the team ("this person needs X from sales to finish the events job").

**As a client report (Knowtific):**
> **Wins:** 23 leads this week (up from 15). Cost per lead down to $38.
> **Next:** Testing 2 new ad videos; fixing the quote form on mobile.
> **Blockers:** Need 5 more before/after photos from recent jobs.
> **Stats:** Spend $870 · Leads 23 · Avg response time 4 min · Site visits 1,240 · Booked jobs 6

Clients who get this stay longer.

## 4. Monthly reporting

Monthly, send a deeper report: social pillars (what's improving, what's being replaced and why), ads (spend, results, best creative, tests next month), funnel (page conversion, close rate, lead volume), SEO/GBP trends, and the plan for next month. Keep it short and decision-focused.

## 5. Starting a new role or client: the 90-day plan

Arrive with a 60–90 day plan, not just ideas: the brand world map you've already sketched, the quick wins in the first 30 days (tracking, landing page fixes, follow-up speed), the tests for days 30–60, and the systems for days 60–90 (pillar system, weekly ad loop, email flows, reporting). Candidates who do this often get hired over others, and clients trust agencies that do it.
