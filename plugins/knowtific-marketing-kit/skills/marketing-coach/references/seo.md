## 1. What SEO is now

Search engine optimisation means pages on your site getting traffic from people searching on Google (or Bing). More people now ask AI tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity, and AI overviews answer some questions without a click, so the pool of clicks is shrinking. But SEO is far from dead. Over years, organic traffic becomes a free generator that keeps going, and it can make or break a business.

- **Only the top 3 results really matter.** Page 2 is nowhere.
- **Brand name matters.** If your name is close to another business in a big category, people click the wrong site and you end up paying for ads on your own brand name every day. Consider search when naming a business or product.

## 2. Tools

- **SEO suites:** Ahrefs and Semrush (paid) analyse a site and show what keywords it ranks for and where, search volume, and a 0–100 difficulty score for each term. SE Ranking is another option.
- **DataForSEO** (pay-as-you-go API with an official MCP) gives keyword volumes, difficulty, live results pages, competitor and backlink data to AI directly. See tools.md.
- **Google Search Console** (free): real queries, clicks, impressions and positions for the client's own site.
- **Google Keyword Planner** (inside Google Ads): volume ranges for free.

## 3. Finding opportunities

There are two main opportunity types:
1. **Specific terms with low competition.** A very specific product or service type people search for ("Japanese acetate sunglasses", "switchboard upgrade Croydon") with decent volume and a low-to-moderate difficulty score. Own it.
2. **Your brand and category terms.** Make sure you own your own name and the obvious category terms in your area.

**Match how people search.** People type "best X", "worst X", "X reviews", "X vs Y", "X near me", "X cost", "how long does X take", and the brand names of competitors. These habits haven't changed, and they now do the same searches on TikTok and Instagram.

## 4. Building pages that rank

For each target term:
1. **A dedicated page** (or collection/category page) focused on that term: the term in the page title and main heading.
2. **A genuinely useful write-up**: what it is, why your business does it, what makes it special, prices or price ranges where possible, FAQs.
3. **Embed a video** about the topic (also helps engagement).
4. **Get links to it** from other websites, social profiles, directories, partners and press. Google weighs how much the rest of the internet points to a page.
5. **Make it satisfying**: Google watches whether people click the result and whether they stay and engage. A page that answers the question well keeps people.

There are "white hat" (legitimate) and "black hat" (manipulative) link-building methods. Use legitimate ones only; black hat risks penalties.

## 5. Timing: do it before you need it

SEO is a war of attrition and takes months, so start before launch. A drink brand launching into a category with little competition was advised to spend 4–6 months on SEO before its big launch. By launch it ranked #1–2 for its category terms. Then a massive influencer seeding campaign drove huge demand, and when people searched, they found the brand. Without that SEO, much of the launch attention would have leaked away. The same organic traffic data later helped the retail pitch ("we get lots of search traffic from this region, but there's nowhere to buy us there").

## 6. Local SEO (Knowtific core)

The videos barely cover this, but it's the biggest lever for local service businesses:
- **Google Business Profile (GBP):** correct primary and secondary categories, service areas, services list with descriptions, hours, photos (real, regularly added), products/services, Q&A, posts. Link it from Google Ads location assets.
- **Reviews:** a steady flow of genuine reviews (ask **every** customer the same way via the job-complete flow), reply to every review. Never buy reviews or offer anything in exchange for one: Google bans incentives even when disclosed, and bans review gating (only asking happy customers or screening first). See australia-compliance.md.
- **Service + suburb pages:** one strong page per core service, and location pages only where the business genuinely serves and has something specific to say (local jobs, photos, testimonials). Avoid thin copy-paste suburb pages.
- **NAP consistency:** name, address and phone identical across the site, GBP and directories (citations: Yellow Pages, Yelp, hipages, True Local, industry directories, Apple Maps, Bing Places).
- **Schema markup:** LocalBusiness (or the specific type), Service, FAQ and Review schema where valid.
- **Technical basics:** fast mobile pages (Core Web Vitals), clean titles and meta descriptions, internal links between services and locations, an XML sitemap submitted to Search Console, no broken pages.
- **Measure:** Search Console clicks and queries, GBP calls/direction requests/website clicks, rankings in the Maps pack for core terms.

## 7. Visibility in AI answers

AI tools recommend businesses when they have a clear reason to. To show up in AI answers:
- Be specific and consistent about what you do, who for and where (positioning that's AI-legible).
- Publish clear, factual pages: services, prices or ranges, service area, credentials, FAQs in plain language.
- Earn mentions on other reputable sites (local news, industry associations, directories, review sites).
- Keep reviews strong and recent; AI tools draw on them.
- Check periodically: ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI "best [service] in [area]" and see who's named and why. DataForSEO has tools for tracking AI mentions.

## 8. Social search

- Instagram posts now appear in Google results, and short-form content keeps getting found for months.
- Title YouTube tutorials the way people search ("How to fix a running toilet in 5 minutes").
- People search TikTok and Instagram for "best X in [city]" too; use searchable words in captions and on-screen text.
