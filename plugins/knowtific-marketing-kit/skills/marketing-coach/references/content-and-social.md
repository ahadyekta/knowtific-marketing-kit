## 1. Why organic social matters

**The alpha.** Social media gives you free attention. A business can post its first video and get hundreds, thousands or millions of views, which wasn't possible a decade ago. It's still underused: many competitors are scared of it, don't know how, or use outdated tactics. That's the advantage.

Organic reach is a function of four things: **narrative** (the story you tell), **taste** (how it looks and feels), **focus** (getting genuinely good at a few techniques) and **credibility** (do people believe you?).

**Where attention comes from (the master plan).** Every business gets new customers from: (1) paid advertising, (2) referrals and word of mouth, (3) cold outreach, and (4) organic social, where you invest time and the reward is uncapped: how far content travels and how long it lasts.

**Short-form is not ephemeral.** Good content keeps getting found for months: Instagram posts now show up in Google search, and seasonal topics resurface every year. Make content with lasting value. No slop, no cheap tricks for views.

**Everything is content.** Ads, TV spots, staff training, event experiences, packaging and unboxing are all content. People film events and unboxings and send them to friends. Plan all of it as content.

**Stay hands-on.** The most useful marketers know platform details: titling YouTube tutorials so they're searchable, tagging related videos, knowing that TikTok brand accounts underperform creator accounts, using multi-account strategies, using Instagram trial reels. A marketing lead shouldn't rely only on the social media person; at least one person (ideally including you) must be in the trenches thinking like a creator.

## 2. Choosing platforms

**Focus on one primary network and get good at it** rather than being average everywhere. Usually that's Instagram, or LinkedIn for B2B. Cross-post to the others.

| Platform | Strengths | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok** | Gives reach fast based on content quality | Business-listed brand accounts struggle. Winning TikTok strategies use an "army": multiple accounts, creators, slideshows, regional or language accounts. Case studies of these tactics: Social Growth Engineers (paid). Without a TikTok specialist, just cross-post from Instagram |
| **Instagram** | Best primary for most businesses; reach from day one; you can change an account's trajectory anytime by posting better content | Posting product photos doesn't work. You need one of the three strategies below |
| **YouTube** | Excellent for service businesses: answer common questions, show expertise. 1,000–2,000 views per video can bring enough quality leads to sustain a business | Needs someone who understands YouTube: thumbnails, titles and positioning, otherwise wasted effort. Title tutorials the way people search |
| **LinkedIn** | B2B and lead-driven businesses. A team sport: works best when 5–10+ people at the company post and engage with each other; some companies run employee programs where most staff participate | One person alone has a much harder time |
| **X, Snapchat** | Add-ons if someone will genuinely engage there | Not a primary for most |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Cross-post for extra reach | Go in with zero expectations of business results |

**For a new account in 2025–26:** focus on TikTok and Instagram (both give free views from post one, have the biggest audience, and content that works on one usually works on the other); add LinkedIn and X if you're willing to engage there.

**Three Instagram strategies (choose one; most businesses should choose expert):**
1. **Expert on camera.** One person talks to camera about the topic: founder, team member or hired creator. The most effective and easiest approach. Needs volume: at least 3 posts a week. The expert can even post from a separate account that promotes the business (e.g. a creator making whiteboard videos about mental health that promote an app).
2. **Storytelling.** The real story of the business as a series (vlogs, carousels), about 2 posts a week, hero's-journey style ("we quit our jobs to take over an old fast-food shop… and today we have a problem"). Only works if the business is genuinely interesting.
3. **Art-directed.** Every post is a conceptual, crafted piece. Needs singular creative talent and a lot of time. Suits fashion, beauty and design brands.

**No face? Use a concept.** Hands-only POV videos with voiceover can work (one brand's Spanish-language POV account reached 800,000 followers). Regional or language versions of the same content can each grow separately. But it must be visually interesting and have an angle.

## 3. Goals, metrics and the content funnel

**Goals:** brand awareness, leads (sign-ups, enquiries, email list), and sales you can attribute.

| Level | Measure |
|---|---|
| Each post | Hook rate (did people keep watching), views, saves, shares (most important: someone sent it to a friend) |
| Account per month | Reach (awareness), leads or sign-ups, attributable sales |

**Content funnel**
- **Top**: gets attention; may not mention the business at all (a fashion founder breaking down great clothing in general). A viral top-of-funnel post raises the floor for all your other posts: the account's baseline views go up.
- **Middle**: educates and builds trust: your expertise, process, collections, how you work.
- **Bottom**: exists to achieve the goal: "I'm the founder; these are my 4 favourite pieces from the new range, get them before they sell out"; "3 spots left this month, link in bio". Usually fewer views, but brings the money.

Use all three. Explain this funnel to bosses and clients so they don't judge every post on views alone.

## 4. Mindset rules

1. **The world beats the brand account.** A brand account can only get so many views, especially without someone on camera. People gravitate to personalities. Experts, founders, hired creators and influencers often drive more impact than the brand account itself, so multiply personalities and put good ones on retainer.
2. **You compete with the whole feed, not your competitors.** Make a fresh account, follow what your ICP (ideal customer profile) follows (look up real customers, or use tools that find customers' social profiles), interact a little, and scroll it 10–15 minutes a day for two weeks. You'll see your real competition: friends, skits, attractive people, funny and relatable content. Your posts must compete with that.
3. **It must be shareable.** Ask: why would someone send this to a friend? Shareable things: relatable experiences, useful expertise, tutorials, funny or surprising content, beautiful visuals. A plain description of your service rarely gets shared.
4. **There's no such thing as a boring business, only boring content.** Army surplus stores, factories, accountants and dentists all have huge accounts somewhere. Until someone makes a category exciting, everyone thinks it's too boring.
5. **Expertise wins right now.** The best-performing branded content in this period has a person or a lens delivering expertise about the category. It suits services (web design, roofing, dentistry) and products that can be made expert-driven. This won't last forever, but it works now.
6. **Thoughtful beats easy.** The content that works is intellectual: it takes real thinking to write. If you look for the easiest answer, you won't make it. Even 600 views is valuable when everyone who watched 30 seconds got real value; those are future customers.
7. **Ignore guru hacks.** "2-second videos", special audios, posting tricks: distractions. Spend the time making the best content in your niche.

**Patterns that work (from real accounts):**
- A K-beauty retailer's founder and team on camera ranking and comparing products ("which suits oily skin?")
- A clothing factory's creator doing quests ("making the best leather jacket in China"), events, techniques, down to "how to work with us" videos
- A web design studio getting almost all its leads by critiquing big brands' websites, then showing its own work. **Directly applicable to Knowtific: "Rating Melbourne tradie websites."**

## 5. Four content types × two formats

| Type | What it is | Example (local) |
|---|---|---|
| **Storytelling** | Your story or great stories in your niche, told as a journey with setup → conflict → struggle → result (the hero's journey) | "Day 1 of renovating a 1960s bathroom in Croydon"; the history of why a certain tile became popular |
| **Ratings and rankings** | Rate or rank things your audience cares about, with candid opinions and reasons | A dentist in the chair rating whitening toothpastes; a baker ranking premium ingredients; a roofer rating cladding types |
| **Breakdowns** | Explain what people need or want to know; give away the playbook; or break down something happening now | An ads specialist walking through a full funnel; a naming expert breaking down a celebrity's new brand name |
| **Scenarios** | Put yourself in a specific situation the viewer is in | "5 exercises to fix knee pain if you're over 40"; "what to do with a 65 m² unit to make it feel bigger"; "how I'd fix this local chain" |

**Two formats for each:** carousel or video. Video can be talking head, edited vlog or green screen. Carousels mean you don't need to be on camera at all, but you must care about design and editing.

**Student or expert perspective.** Either you're the expert ("I'm a dentist, here's how it works") or you're the student learning in public ("I bought every toothpaste to find out which is best"). Both work. Choose deliberately.

**Look the part.** The dentist in the dentist's chair, the baker in the kitchen in whites, the electrician at the switchboard. It signals credibility instantly.

**Don't fear giving it away.** Breakdowns that reveal everything don't stop people hiring you. Most viewers won't do it themselves; the ones who would hire you just want proof you know what you're doing. Those who do it themselves become advocates.

## 6. Twelve angles for "boring" businesses

1. **Format shakeup.** Take a famous format and remix it: a spicy-wings interview show becomes "hot takes on legal questions while eating hot wings". There are hundreds of formats to borrow.
2. **Speed.** Do the normal thing much faster: a 10-second speedrun apartment tour, 30-second makeup, a one-minute recipe. The algorithm likes pace, and doing it fast becomes an event.
3. **Viral vehicle.** One object that makes every video interesting: a toy store with a hydraulic press, a vintage car, a striking location. A one-off purchase that keeps paying off, and you become known for it.
4. **"This is amazing."** Use your skill on something delightful: a copywriting agency bringing romance novels to life in 15-second videos.
5. **Exciting scenarios.** Put your expertise inside something people already find exciting: an accountant breaking down the tax maths of buying a luxury 4WD, or what a sports star's interstate move does to their tax.
6. **Strong takes.** Brave, clear opinions from someone credible ("never use this grey in a kitchen"). People argue in the comments, and that's attention. Hot takes about your own industry, and "what I would have done" critiques of others' work, are especially strong.
7. **Current events and pop culture (attention hijacking).** A therapist analysing reality TV characters; a lawyer explaining what rap lyrics would mean in court; a barista making "the coffee I'd make for [celebrity]". Take something people already care about and apply your expertise.
8. **Questions people feel dumb asking.** Answer what everyone pretends to know.
9. **Versus.** "X vs Y": compare two things and give your verdict. A dry educator blew up doing "is this education or edutainment?"
10. **Lore and depth.** Be the biggest nerd in the niche: inside jokes, history, obscure details, memes. An army surplus store became huge by going deep on military history. Hooks like "I went to the Coachella of dermatologists" make a niche event exciting.
11. **Normal content plus one interesting element.** Roofing tips filmed on a ladder; everything shot from one striking angle. If content is failing, it's often missing that one thing.
12. **Tour guide to a hidden world.** Don't sell the product; sell access to a world people have never seen: the factory district, the trade show, behind the scenes of the industry. Then you become the people to call when someone needs that world.

## 7. Hooks, mechanisms and outlines

**A hook has three parts:** text on screen, the visual, and the first spoken line. Plan all three.

**Mechanisms (styles):**
- **Blitz**: fast lists ("10 habits of…", "5 mistakes in 30 seconds")
- **Storytime**: longer personal stories, even several minutes
- **Versus, ranking, reaction, quest, tutorial, POV, street interview, one-shot, single photo**

**Simple outlines** keep new creators on track:
- Controversial question → rapid-fire opinions → justifications → punch line
- Hook → scenario → point 1 about the product → joke → call to action
- Hook → problem → how we solved it → result → CTA

**Finding ideas.** Video databases and idea tools let you browse by format and topic, sort by outliers (posts performing 2× or more above an account's normal), and see hook breakdowns (title, verbal, visual) and narrative frameworks. Look outside your niche: formats travel. Social Growth Engineers covers fringe tactics in odd niches.

**Social shows.** Episodic series (like a short TV show playing out on Instagram) are popular and great for quiet periods.

## 8. Starting or fixing an account

**Setup**
- Use a **creator account**, not a business account, on TikTok and Instagram (business accounts tend to get less reach; Instagram's creator account still gives you insights and a contact button). Exception: if you sell through TikTok Shop, many brands keep the shop on a business account and post videos from a separate creator account.
- **Start the account in the country you want to reach**, with a local phone number, and post mostly from there. An account started on holiday overseas can end up shown to the wrong country. If you can't, hire someone local (e.g. on Upwork) to set it up and keep access yourself.
- **Post natively.** One large group test found schedulers (including the platforms' own) reduced performance. Treat this as an observation worth testing, but the safe default is to upload directly. Cross-posting takes 2–3 minutes per platform; just do it.
- Ads can still run on Instagram regardless of account type.

**Cadence for a new or reset account**
1. Choose 2–3 combinations of type × format (e.g. ratings-video, breakdown-carousel, scenario-video).
2. Post 2 of each per week: **4–6 posts a week**. Too busy? Pick two and do four posts.
3. Run each for a full month (about 4 of each) before judging anything.
4. Layer on content answering the common questions that come up in comments ("everyone asks about colour correction → next video"). As a student, go find the answer and report back.
5. Keep going for at least three months.

## 9. The pillar system and growing

**Level 1: the pillar system**
- Minimum team: a social media manager and someone on camera (sometimes the same person).
- Choose **3–5 formats** to test; these are your pillars (e.g. carousels, vlogs, sit-down explainers).
- Post each pillar **3–4 times a month** (12–16 posts).
- Once a month, a few days after the month ends, compare pillars on views, saves, shares, hook rate and new vs existing followers, and compare month over month. Is each improving or getting worse?
- If a pillar underperforms for **two months** in a row, replace it.
- Send a short monthly report to the boss or client: what we posted, top performers, what's improving, what we're changing.
- It takes about an hour a month. Use `pillar_report.py` (in the social-plan skill) to do the maths.
- The system lets you **validate or kill ideas with data**: when the boss's cousin says "we should do videos like this", it becomes a pillar for a month and the numbers decide. After 4–5 months you have a data-backed playbook you can show the next client.
- Expect traction in **3–5 months**; even a weak start improves into good content by month 6.

**Cross-post everything** to Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook, LinkedIn and X. It takes minutes and compounds over time.

**Level 2: once you're good at one network**
- If content works across networks, keep cross-posting. If not, build a separate strategy for the second network (e.g. TikTok needs more candid, phone-shot content, maybe two creators on the account).
- Add a **collaborator pillar**: a regular creator makes about 4 videos a month as collab posts, bringing in their audience (common for restaurants and food accounts).
- Don't add networks until you're good at one.

## 10. Calls to action, engagement and DMs

- **No CTAs until you have traction** (around 5,000+ views per post; about 2,000 in small niches like construction or SEO). Asking too early hurts reach.
- Then drive to a **lead magnet** or advertorial page: via the link in bio, or with comment-to-DM automation (ManyChat or similar: "comment GUIDE and we'll DM you the link").
- **Reply to every comment and every DM** personally.
- **DM proactively.** If someone keeps commenting and looks like a real prospect (right area, homeowner, works at a target company), message them: "Saw you've been checking out our posts. Thinking about a new roof? What suburb are you in?" Social media is a sales tool, and it's *social*.
- If you won't do it yourself, have the sales team in the DMs, responding fast and booking calls.
- Treat any commercial DMs you send carefully under the Spam Act (see australia-compliance.md).

## 11. Production: batching, sets and people

- **Batch filming.** Most companies prepare scripts and prompts, then shoot with the on-camera person once every two weeks.
- **Content in every stage.** Each piece should be reused on the landing page, in pre-call emails, on the sales call, after the call, and in email automations. A video with a few thousand views can still be valuable if it does a job in the funnel. Plan content as part of the whole marketing system, not as a side budget.
- **Level up the set.** A distinctive, credible backdrop is the multiplier: books and a candle, a wall of fragrances behind a fragrance founder, a tidy workshop with tools. Even a "student" can show the collection they've built so far. Sets are a major creator trend.
- **The person matters most.** An expert-based business on social needs a person front and centre: founder, team member or hired creator. Faceless carousels and voiceover videos can work, but it's harder.
