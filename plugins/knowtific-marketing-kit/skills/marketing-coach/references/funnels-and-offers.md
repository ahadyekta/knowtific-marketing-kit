## 1. What a funnel is

A funnel is the journey a potential customer takes from first seeing you to buying. It's called a funnel because people drop off at each step: lots see a post or ad (top), fewer visit the site, fewer view the offer, fewer enquire or add to cart, and a few buy.

```
ATTENTION → CTA → LANDING PAGE → LEAD or SALE → CRM → FOLLOW-UP → CUSTOMER → REPEAT/REFERRAL
```

Good marketers analyse every stage two ways: **what marketing happens at that level** (awareness campaigns, influencers, ads, retargeting, emails), and **how to lose as few people as possible** at that step. Traffic is precious: you paid for it with money or effort, so every visitor should get the right message, images and next step.

**Where attention comes from:** ads, organic social, referrals (organic or a referral program), search (Google, and now ChatGPT and AI answers), and foot traffic (people walking past a shop, or past your product in a retailer's aisle).

## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Landing page** | The page people land on from an ad, post or bio link. Not your homepage; built for one action |
| **Lead** | Someone who gave contact details but hasn't bought. Stored in a **CRM** (customer relationship manager, e.g. HubSpot), with records of where they came from and every contact. CRMs hold two record types: leads and customers |
| **Lead magnet** | Something valuable given in exchange for contact details: a checklist ("12 steps to prepare for your home renovation"), a price guide, a free quote or estimate, a diet plan, a report. For expensive services with long sales cycles, an in-depth report or ebook can bring in thousands of leads; only a handful need to convert over the next 6–12 months, nurtured by email until they're ready. You can build a list before you even know what you'll sell |
| **Squeeze page** | A landing page whose only job is to get the lead magnet sign-up, with as high a conversion rate as possible |
| **Thank-you page** | The page after sign-up or purchase. Usually wasted; should carry the next step (see section 6) |
| **Upsell** | Offering something bigger straight after a small purchase or free sign-up ("add a subscription", "add the audit", "turn this into a retainer", "free cleaning for $99 with this") |
| **Extras / bonus** | Things included to sweeten the offer (a course, community access, a maintenance check). When people aren't buying, test adding extras |
| **Guarantee** | Reduces risk: "if we don't finish in 10 days, you get $X back". Optional but powerful (must comply with consumer law) |
| **Listicle page** | An article-style page ("6 reasons it's time to replace your hot water system") that educates, then asks |
| **Setter / closer** | Founders usually close calls themselves first. As volume grows, a **closer** runs sales calls and a **setter** books calls, sends reminders and makes sure people show up |
| **Automation** | Emails and SMS sent automatically after an action: sign-up, abandoned cart, quote sent |
| **Top / bottom of funnel** | Top = getting attention and traffic. Bottom = people ready to buy. A **qualified lead** fits (right company or home, budget, area, need) and has shown enough interest. Hot qualified leads deserve extra attention and separate marketing |
| **DM funnel** | Selling through direct messages: starting conversations with engaged followers and commenters and managing them in a CRM. Some businesses run hundreds of DM conversations at once |

## 3. Offers

A vague offer ("I do graphic design") doesn't sell. Use the formula:

> **I'm a [type of provider] that does [specific thing] for [specific customer].**

Examples: "A graphic designer that makes landing pages for creators." "An interior designer that does home renovations in Melbourne's bayside suburbs." "An electrician that upgrades switchboards for older homes in Melbourne's east."

Then shape the offer with: price point, what's included, extras/bonuses, guarantee, urgency (genuine), and the next step (call, quote, booking).

**When a call is needed.** For anything above roughly $2,000, people usually need to talk to someone before buying. Most service businesses fall here, so the funnel's goal is a booked call, not a sale.

## 4. Calls to action and the bio

A **CTA** tells people exactly what to do next. No CTA means people watch and leave.
- Sales: "Buy now", "Get 20% off before it's gone", "Join the waitlist"
- Leads: "Get a free quote", "Download the free cheat sheet", "Book a free 15-minute consult"

**Soft CTAs make hard CTAs work.** Many businesses push traffic to the site but only offer "buy" or "call us". Asking for an email or phone number in exchange for something useful (free video series, guide, discount code) keeps more people in the funnel and gets them closer to buying later. Put opt-in sections on key pages, and offer real value, not "sign up for our newsletter".

**The three-line social bio:**
1. What the business does
2. Why follow (what you'll learn or get)
3. The CTA, with a link

> Plumbing & hot water, Melbourne east 🔧
> Tips to save money on plumbing problems
> 👇 Get a free quote in 2 minutes

## 5. Landing pages

**Why they matter.** Spend $100 to send 100 people at $1 a click:
| Page converts at | Leads | Cost per lead |
|---|---|---|
| 1% | 1 | $100 |
| 4% | 4 | $25 |
| 12% | 12 | about $8 |

Same ads, same budget, 12× different results. The landing page is marketing's property.

**Default first, variants later.** Starting out (spending $50–$100 a day), focus on your main pages. As spend grows and you find different audiences, build page variants per audience or campaign: different photos (people aged 30+ vs 50+), the creators from that campaign, different messages.

**Hard-CTA page essentials:** call out the objective the visitor is trying to achieve, the benefits to them (not just specs), testimonials and reviews, and an FAQ that answers objections. **Answer every buying objection on the page:** how does it fit (show it on different body types)? What's it made of? Can I use it if I have a condition? Do I need to move out during the renovation?

### Type 1: product detail page (PDP) structure
1. Lifestyle photo/video carousel (not just glamour shots), with the option to buy the full set or routine up top
2. Real results on real people (an example for every option)
3. Benefits reframed for the buyer: what it's good for, what it feels and looks like
4. Value propositions and how it works
5. How to use it
6. Ingredients, materials or specs, in full
7. Data and proof: studies, consumer results, graphs, timelines
8. The people behind it
9. Lots of testimonials
10. FAQ for objections

Variations that work: a short video for each feature; comparison charts against alternatives; goal-based sections; customer video sections; "pairs well with"; quizzes for choosing the right option.

### Type 2: service page structure (Knowtific default)
1. **Hero:** specific outcome headline, subheadline with the key difference, 2–3 benefits, primary CTA, trust signal (rating, licence, years)
2. **Real results:** before/after gallery of local jobs, or results (for health: process and experience, not clinical outcomes)
3. **Why customers choose us:** 3–4 benefits from the customer's view
4. **How it works:** 3–4 simple steps
5. **What's included**
6. **Proof:** numbers, reviews, case study
7. **Meet the team:** owner photo, story, licence or registration number
8. **Reviews:** Google reviews, video testimonials (where allowed)
9. **FAQ:** the real objections
10. **Final CTA** with response-time promise ("we reply within 1 hour")

### Home page (local service business)
The home page is for people who already know the name (referrals, Google Business Profile, van signage) and need to confirm they're in the right place. It's not the page to send ads to; ads go to a dedicated service or landing page. (This structure is Knowtific's default, built from the service page below.)
1. **Hero:** what you do, for whom and where ("Hot water repairs and replacements across Melbourne's east, same day"), proof line (rating, reviews count, licence, years), call button + quote button
2. **Services:** cards for each core service, each linking to its own service page
3. **Why us:** 3–4 specific differences (not "quality and reliable")
4. **Recent work:** before/after or a project gallery with suburb names
5. **Reviews:** a few real Google reviews with names and suburbs, plus the overall rating
6. **How it works:** 3 steps from first contact to job done
7. **Service area:** suburbs or a map; links to genuine location pages
8. **Team / owner:** photo, short story, licence or registration number
9. **FAQ:** pricing approach, call-out fees, timing, warranties
10. **Final CTA** with response-time promise; footer with ABN, licence, address/area, hours, privacy policy

### Type 3: listicle page
Teaches first, then sells: "14 steps to this look", "7 mistakes Melbourne homeowners make when renovating a bathroom", with the business woven through and a CTA at the end. Good for people not ready to buy, and for content-driven ads.

### Type 4: luxury page
The question is "why is it worth this much?" Your job is to impress: design inspiration, the process from sketch onward, macro close-up shots of craft and materials, high-quality video people will sit and watch, lifestyle, then specs, with an "inquire" CTA. By the time they reach the features, the price should make sense.

**Build a swipe file.** Every time you click an ad, note the landing page's sections in order. Save screenshots in a folder. You'll build a library of structures to test.

## 6. After the lead

**Speed to touch.** Contact new leads within 1–5 minutes; every minute of delay reduces the chance they respond. Use instant SMS/email acknowledgement, a notification to the person who'll call, or an AI agent that replies and books. (This is what Knowtific's follow-up engine is built for.)

**Thank-you page.** Massively underrated. Instead of "thanks, we'll be in touch":
- A short video from the person who'll call: who they are and what happens next
- The steps of the process
- Proof (reviews, best videos)
- Optional upsell: "Want to book your consult now?"

**"Prepare for your call" page.** After someone books, a confirmation page: who they'll meet (photo, short intro, their social profile), the agenda ("first 10 minutes we'll cover…"), what to have ready. Far more people show up, and the call isn't a mystery.

**Close assets** (sent before and after the call, or in automations): best videos, testimonials and reviews ("here are 50 people who got results"; do their research for them), FAQ and objection answers (PDF, email or video), case studies. See sales.md.

**Email after sign-up:** an immediate email confirming what they signed up for, introducing the person behind it and what to expect; on day 2, your most popular content on that topic; then keep nurturing and educating. Get at least those first two right.

**The list is an asset.** An email list of people interested in your field is a lifetime asset: you can launch any future offer to it. Start building it early and keep it going.

**Map it with the sales team.** Sit down with whoever answers the phone and map the flow: where leads come from, what page they see, what they get after, what objections come up, and what content would help. Sales starts loving marketing.

## 7. Funnel types

| Funnel | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead magnet funnel** | Free resource → sign-up → thank-you page with an upsell (book a consult) → confirmation/"prepare for your call" page → email automation | Most service businesses; also lets you put numbers on social media (leads and list growth) |
| **Tripwire funnel** | A cheap first purchase ($1–$99), then an immediate higher-value offer. The first purchase breaks the psychological barrier. Can **fund ad spend**: if ads for a $99 product break even, every buyer is a free lead for a $5,000 service | Agencies with an entry service, info products, e-commerce loss leaders |
| **Webinar funnel** | Landing page → registration → one-to-many live presentation with Q&A → offer. Reminders by email and SMS | When you can't do one-on-one calls at volume, or have a charismatic presenter |
| **Video sales letter (VSL)** | One long persuasive video does most of the selling. Typical beats: a specific problem → a unique framework or method → why this person is the right one to solve it → objections handled → CTA | Offers that need a lot of explanation, presenters good on video |

## 8. Testing and building pages

**Test variants:** long vs short pages, light vs dark mode, with or without video, and three copy angles: human-driven narrative, evidence-driven, and direct response.

**Building with AI.** AI page builders (e.g. Framer's agent) can now generate layouts from a description or a screenshot and fill in your styles. Knowtific's equivalent: Claude Code in the client's Next.js repo, with a brand design skill (colours, fonts, button styles, image style, spacing) so every new page matches. Feed it a swipe-file screenshot plus the client's content and ask for a draft, then critique ("lighter", "move the buttons", "check mobile") and deploy. The building is commoditised; the value is the idea, the offer and the structure.

**Free and paid templates** give a visual starting point; add your copy and assets.

## 9. The numbers that matter

Obsess over two:
1. **Landing page conversion rate**: % of visitors who become leads or buyers.
2. **Close rate**: % of leads who become customers.

Also watch: **lead volume**, and (advanced) **qualified rate**: % of leads who are actually good prospects. Improve close rate with faster responses, booking reminders, better thank-you and preparation pages, and stronger close assets. Use `funnel_math.py` (funnel-build skill) to model what each improvement is worth.
